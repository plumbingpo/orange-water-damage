Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Orange

Drywall acts like a wick. When water reaches the bottom of a wall, the gypsum and paper draw it upward, often a foot or more above the level the water ever reached on the floor. The painted face can look and feel normal while the back side and the insulation behind it are soaked. Looks cannot be trusted here, so every wall gets tested with a meter.

Plenty of walls that get wet can stay. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. If the drywall has gone soft, crumbles, shows mold or took in dirty water, we do a flood cut, meaning a level cut over the damaged area with all material beneath it removed.

Warning Signs

  • Swollen or warped baseboards with a gap opening behind them
  • New gaps along casings and baseboard tops as swollen material shifts
  • Cool, slightly clammy drywall next to walls at normal temperature
  • Wall coverings lifting, blistering or creasing close to floor level
  • Drywall seams ridging up and screw pops appearing across the surface
  • Staining in a horizontal band just above the baseboard

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Walls You Cannot See

Wet drywall behind cabinets, vanities and built-ins gets no airflow and is easy to overlook. We take readings through toe kicks and from the opposite side to find it.

Water Next Door

The neighboring room or closet gets wet as water travels below the wall plate. We check both sides of every affected wall and the flooring along it.

Moisture in Exterior Walls

On an exterior wall, water can soak the sheathing behind the insulation. That layer dries slowly and is checked before the wall is closed.

Soaked Batts in the Wall

Batts of fiberglass absorb water and keep it pressed to the framing. No amount of airflow on the painted side will dry a cavity packed with wet batts.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Wall and Drywall Water Damage

1

Map the Moisture

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

Based on the water category, how long it sat and how the drywall feels, we tell you plainly which sections can be dried in place and which need to come out.

3

Open Up or Air Out

For walls being saved, baseboards come off and air is directed into the cavity. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on, usually 3 to 5 days, until the studs and the drywall left in place test dry. After that comes insulation, new drywall hung and finished, trim put back and paint.

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Water Starts Climbing

Moisture enters where the panel meets the floor and wicks upward from there. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

24 to 48 hours

Swelling and Blisters

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Inside the cavity, the paper face becomes a place mold can begin.

Day 3 to 7

Panels Lose Strength

The gypsum core turns soft and its paper starts to separate. Within the wall the insulation sags, a smell develops and mold moves over the back of the panel.

After a week

Damage Reaches the Studs

By this point the studs and plates have been damp long enough for mold and early rot. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.
