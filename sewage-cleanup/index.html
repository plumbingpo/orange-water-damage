A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. Across Orange, Orange Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Hair and other debris are easy to stop with simple strainers on tub, shower and sink drains
  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues
  • Stormwater from a sump or downspout should never drain into the sewer, since the extra volume invites a backup
  • Address slow drains promptly, before they become complete blockages
  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it
  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects

Why Hire a Professional for Sewage Cleanup?

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Strong sewage or rotten egg odors coming from drains or lower levels
  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs
  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors
  • More than one drain in the house overflowing or backing up together
  • Soggy, smelly ground over the sewer line or leakage at the cleanout cap
